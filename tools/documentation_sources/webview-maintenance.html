Historical fork: why it existed and what it preserved

A local development copy remains outside the buyer package. Its pubspec identifies webview_flutter_android 3.12.0. Source inspection shows a concrete custom bridge for an earlier embedded integration; the broad old claim about unspecified media/lifecycle fixes did not explain it.

  • WebViewHostApiImpl.loadUrl called setNativeBridge, registering a JavaScript interface named NativeBridge.
  • The interface exposed getConfig, destroy, reportGameSize, gameRecharge, gameLoaded and sendGameAction. Each forwarded a string through a main-thread Handler to BSEventChannel. The bridge's destroy method forwarded a message; it did not itself destroy the native WebView.
  • BSEventChannel registered the EventChannel baishunChannel, managed its event sink on listen/cancel, and forwarded messages to Dart.
  • WebViewFlutterPlugin registered that channel on engine attachment and detached its stream handler on engine removal.

These facts explain the historical fork's observable custom behavior. They are not instructions to restore the removed integration. No separately verified patch history establishing custom media playback fixes was supplied, so this guide does not claim them.
